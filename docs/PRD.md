# Product

Who this is for, what it does, and what it deliberately does not do.

---

## The product

**mskool** — a multi-tenant SaaS School Management System for private schools in India:
CBSE, ICSE, state board, and unaffiliated.

The buyer is a **Trust or Society** that owns one or more school branches. That is why the
tenant is the organization, not the school (ADR-001) — a trust running four branches wants
one account, one bill, one set of staff records, and cross-branch reporting.

Positioned as production infrastructure, not an MVP. Schools run payroll-adjacent,
fee-collection, and legally-significant record-keeping on this. Data loss or a wrong
receipt number is not a bug report, it is a customer-ending event. That is where the 12
hard rules come from.

---

## Personas

**Owner / Trustee** — org-scoped, sees every branch. Cares about collection rates,
enrollment trends, and comparisons between branches. Rarely enters data. Technically
`org_admin` at org scope (ADR-011).

**Principal** — one branch. Approves concessions, publishes results, overrides attendance,
resolves the exceptions everyone else escalates.

**Teacher** — marks attendance, enters marks for assigned subjects and sections. The most
frequent user, often on a phone, often in a two-minute window between classes. Their
access is bounded by `section_teacher_assignments` (ADR-012).

**Accountant / Front office** — collects fees, issues receipts, chases dues, handles
bounced cheques and refunds. Lives in the fees module all day; it must be fast and
keyboard-driven.

**Admin staff** — admissions, student records, transfer certificates, guardian details.

**Parent** — the real user of the student portal. Checks fees, results, and attendance;
pays online. Logs in with a phone number (ADR-007); one login covers all their children
(ADR-008). Guardians have no separate account — the student account *is* the family
account (ADR-006).

**Student** — the account is theirs on paper. Older students check results and homework
themselves; for younger ones the parent is the only real user. Write the portal UI for an
adult.

## Scope

**Core, in build order** (see `docs/TASKS.md` for phasing):

1. **Foundation** — orgs, schools, staff, students, guardians, authorization
2. **Academic structure** — years, terms, classes, sections, subjects, enrollment
3. **Attendance** — daily or period-wise, corrections, summaries
4. **Fees** — structures, concessions, installments, collection, receipts, ledger
5. **Exams & results** — components, grading, computation, report cards
6. **Student portal** — profile, attendance, results, fee payment

**Deferred but planned:** homework (ADR-014), notifications/WhatsApp, timetabling,
admissions workflow, transport, library, GST invoicing, Tally export.

## Deliberately not built

Not a case of "we ran out of time" — each of these is a decision.

**No guardian logins.** One account per family, reached through the student record. Every
alternative multiplies accounts (two parents × three children = six logins to provision
and reset) for a marginal gain. Revisit only if custody-split access becomes a real
customer requirement.

**No OTP login.** Per-login SMS cost, and an entire delivery-failure support surface, for
a system parents open a few times a month. Phone plus password, with staff-driven resets
(ADR-007).

**No row-level security.** Tenancy is enforced in the service layer via required
`DataScope` arguments (hard rule 1), which is checkable at compile time. RLS would be a
second enforcement mechanism to keep in sync with the first.

**No hard deletes, anywhere.** See hard rule 2.

**No native mobile app yet.** The web portal is mobile-first. When a native app is
justified, the REST surface generated by `trpc-to-openapi` is already there.

**No timetabling in v1.** It is a genuinely hard constraint-solving problem and an
independent domain. Doing it badly is worse than not doing it — schools have working
spreadsheets.

**No accounting system.** `financial_transactions` is a fee ledger, not double-entry
books. Schools have Tally; the plan is to export to it, not replace it.

## What "done" looks like for the core

- A trust can onboard, create branches, and invite staff with scoped roles
- Staff can admit students, assign sections, and generate subject enrollments
- Teachers can mark attendance on a phone in under a minute per section
- The office can collect a fee, issue a sequential receipt, and reconcile the day's
  collection against the ledger
- Teachers can enter marks; a principal can publish; parents see results only after
  publication
- Parents can log in with a phone number, see all their children, and pay online
- Year-end: results compute, students promote into new enrollment rows, unpaid dues carry
  forward as opening balances

Each of those is a full vertical slice — schema, service, router, and UI — not a
checkbox on a feature list.
